Chapter 0 — the fun one

The playground

Everything the Python renderer does, distilled into your browser. Watch a single orbit walk (Alan-Becker style), let it rain orbits until a Buddha condenses out of the mist, zoom the Mandelbrot set, or write your own formula and see what its escapees look like. Nothing is pre-rendered here — every pixel is computed live, on your machine, right now.

1 · Orbit theater

One starting point, one rule, frame by frame. The faint blue shading marks c-values whose orbits survive 60+ iterations (the map’s “prisoner country”); the orange thread is the orbit you’re growing. Pick c on the canvas, or drive it from the explorer below.

Rain of orbits

Two thousand simultaneous escaping orbits, glowing additively — a miniature Buddhabrot condensing in front of you. Click to restart the rain somewhere new.

2 · The developing room

The actual Buddhabrot algorithm, at 360², accumulating forever. Each frame adds thousands of sampled orbits; the tone map re-anchors as exposure grows — exactly like the Python renderer’s percentile technique, live. Give it thirty seconds and the figure emerges.

What you are watching

Phase 1 and phase 2 of the renderer, fused: each sampled c is classified on the fly, and if it escapes, its whole path is dropped into the exposure. The grain you see fading is the 1/√N law from the math chapter, visible in real time.

3 · The explorer

Classic escape-time Mandelbrot with 4× supersampling. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to reset — and click (without dragging) to send that c straight into the orbit theater above.

    6 · Experiments — mutate, pin, share

    The laboratory notebook. Every experiment — map or operator grammar, constants, caps, window, tone gamma — serializes to a compact URL hash, so a copied link lands anyone on exactly your state. Surprise me composes a random valid grammar from a seed (same seed, same map), Mutate this perturbs exactly one operator of the current map and shows it side-by-side with its base, and the suggestion engine scores seeded candidates by escape fraction and dynamic range. Pin favourites to localStorage, export/import them as JSON, save any experiment as JSON or PNG, and export the current experiment as an APRS bundle (.aprs.json, the portable Attractor Parameter Record Set of the collection) or load one back — validated in-browser against the same field set as scripts/validate_aprs.py. Everything offline, no server, works straight off a file:// copy.
