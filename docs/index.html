Orbit-density fractals

Long-exposure photographs of
mathematical escape

The Mandelbrot set asks “does this point escape?”. The Buddhabrot asks “where does it travel on the way out?” — and keeps the answer.

Every image here is a histogram of where millions of escaping orbits actually went. No raytracing, no painting: one line of iteration z ↦ z² + c, a few hundred million samples, and a camera made of counting.

The collection

Seventeen maps, one renderer. Each keeps the same skeleton — sample, iterate, classify, replay, histogram, tone-map — and swaps the beating heart: the update rule. Click any card to open it live in the playground.

How this project grew

One session, five acts — recorded here because the process turned out to be as interesting as the pictures.

  • ACT I — the classic
    A two-phase numpy renderer: classify who escapes, then replay only the escapees and histogram every point they visit. 74 million samples, three iteration caps (80 / 400 / 1500) for R/G/B. 3 min 05 s on a laptop CPU. The tone curve needed a real black-point anchor — log-compression alone turned the first attempt into fog.
  • ACT II — the first mutation
    x′ = cos(x²) − y, y′ = 2xy. Scouting revealed two laws: large |x| folds back through the cosine within one step (so y is the true exit direction — escape is judged on |y|), and the lone −y term breaks mirror symmetry: a lopsided Buddha (0.74 vs 0.95 correlation). ~17% of starts never escape.
  • ACT III — the farm
    One generalized renderer, seventeen update rules, four parallel shards. Canvases follow the maps: sin/cos brots are banners (prisoners confined to a horizontal band), the cosfull mutation is a 1:3 tower (91% of orbit points inside |y|<3, journeys to |y|=100).
  • ACT IV — the bug
    Sparse renders triggered an investigation. The test suite caught it: a diverged orbit can overflow to NaN before the chunked escape-check fires, and NaN comparisons are False — so the point masquerades as a prisoner forever. Two-thirds of the escaping orbits in the quadratic family had been silently dropped. One guard ((r > 4) | ~isfinite(r)) and 200M corrective samples later, the family is whole. Full story →
  • ACT V — the museum
    Numeric QC (symmetry correlation, center-of-mass, region brightness, coverage), a test suite of 24 assertions, CI on three Python versions, and this site: a playground where the maths runs in your browser.

The philosophy of the Buddhabrot

It is a photograph

A long exposure. Each sample c is a photon; its orbit is the streak it draws on the film; the histogram is the film itself, counting hits. The Antibuddhabrot is the negative — the photons that never leave the room. The Nebulabrot is a longer exposure still: iteration caps of 5000 separate the travelers who merely linger from those who are truly lost.

It requires prophecy

You cannot draw an escaping orbit while it runs — you don’t yet know it will escape. The picture only exists after classification, which is why the renderer works in two phases: first judge every point by its entire future, then re-run only the ones whose story is worth telling. The image is a memory of futures.

Symmetry is law

Maps with real coefficients obey conjugation equivariance: mirror the input, mirror the output — so the Buddha stands symmetric. Fold an absolute value into the rule (Burning Ship, Buffalo) or a lone −y (our mutations) and the figure tips over, because the law is broken on purpose. Every render is QC-checked against the symmetry its formula predicts.

Noise is the medium

The image is a Monte-Carlo estimate; its grain obeys shot noise, fading as 1/√N. The tone curve — log compression between percentile anchors — is the darkroom technique that decides which truths survive. Turn the knobs yourself in the Tone Lab.
