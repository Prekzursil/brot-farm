## Estimator (the one chosen option of the lane) and its unbiasedness

S3 = single-sample randomized-level truncation applied to the study's
estimand. Target per orbit c (identical object to the study's S0 at cap C,
`conv_samplers` plot-before-step semantics, in-frame points only):
A(c) = 1[t(c) <= C] * sum_{i=1..t(c)} 1[in-frame](z_i) * delta_cell(z_i).
Per orbit: draw level H in {2..C} ~ p; run the orbit; t == 1 -> contribute
z_1 with weight 1 (base term); t == H -> contribute z_1..z_t with weight
1/p_H (level term); else contribute nothing.

3-line derivation (levels 2..C; A_h - A_{h-1} = 1[t=h] sum_{i<=h} d_i for
h >= 2):
  E[contrib|c] = 1[t=1] d_{z1} + sum_{h=2..C} p_h * 1[t=h] (sum_{i<=h} d_i)/p_h
              = 1[t=1] d_{z1} + sum_{h=2..C} 1[t=h] sum_{i<=h} d_i
              = 1[t<=C] sum_{i<=t} d_i = A(c).   (exact, for ANY full-support p)

Level law (pilot-shaped, exactness-independent): on pilot-observed levels
p_h ∝ sqrt((N(h)+0.5) * m_h^2 / h) — the compute-constrained variance
optimum for orthogonal level increments D_h (Var = sum_h E|D_h|^2 / p_h,
cost E[H] = sum_h p_h h; minimizing Var x cost gives p ∝ sqrt(E D^2 / h)
by Cauchy-Schwarz); N(h) = pilot count of orbits escaping exactly at h
(2M-draw pilot per map, seed BASE3+7+map_idx), m_h = their mean in-frame
point count (linear interp; slope-1 extrapolation capped at h). Levels
with no pilot escape share total mass 1e-4 spread ∝ m_h (full support is
required for exactness). Pilot E[H]: mandel 451.4, cosmut 394.5 (draws run
deep, but realized cost is E[min(H,t)]: measured 37.5 vs S0's 50.0
evals/draw on mandel, 70.6 vs 215.6 on cosmut — S3 is CHEAPER per draw on
both maps and still loses everywhere, which is the strong form of the
refutation).

Numerical unbiasedness validation (`results/probe_s3_validate.json`):

## Why it fails (the structural result worth keeping)

The Rhee-Glynn/Misso machinery buys unbiasedness by paying a variance
premium on the level increments D_h. It is profitable when the increments
DECAY with the level, so that the premium sum sum_h sqrt(E|D_h|^2) stays
comparable to the fixed-horizon variance. For escape-time orbit density
the increments do NOT decay: A_h - A_{h-1} is the ENTIRE trajectory of an
orbit escaping exactly at h — m_h points, growing ~linearly in h — while
P(t = h) collapses. On mandel/1500 the pilot has 365k escapes at t=2 but
only 1 at t=1200, and m_1200 = 1196 (the trajectory stays in frame), so
E|D_h|^2 = P m^2 is flat-to-rising across four decades of h (0.54 at h=2
vs 0.72 at h=1200): the premium sum never converges, so (i) any level law
either wastes draws on deep levels that
never hit (E[H] -> cap: the naive sqrt(P)*m law gave E[H]=805 at alpha=0.05
— measured in the pilot sweep) or (ii) starves them and eats weight spikes
(max weight grows with N: 681 -> 1458 on mandel, 543 -> 2055 on cosmut
from N=62.5k to 4M — the tail is not sampled to convergence even at 4M).
The S3 error plateau (mandel mean rel err 1.90 -> 1.38 from 62.5k to 4M)
is weight-spike noise, not MC noise: slope -0.076. S0's error is pure MC
noise: slope -0.461. Randomized truncation trades a decaying bias for a
non-decaying variance here — the opposite of the regime where it wins.

## Why b(S3) flags skew under the study's S0-calibrated test

The study's bias rule scales |b(X)| by SE from an S0 control pair — valid
for S2 (whose b is real skew, reproduced this session: -0.408 mandel,
-0.0995 cosmut, 88.9x/2958.8x SE) and for S0 (|b|/SE = 0.45/32.0).
Applied to S3 it flags variance as skew: S3 is per-orbit exactly unbiased
(cap-40 z-test above), but its b spreads across seeds by +-0.09 (mandel)
on an SE scale of 0.0046. An S0-pair SE is simply not the right scale for
a 200x-worse-variance estimator; the per-seed b values are recorded so the
reader can apply either reading. This does not change the verdict: the
lane's criteria are slope and bias/SE-at-matched-budget, and S3 clears
neither.

The `b` that enters `|b|/SE` is the per-run signed mean, `analyze.signed_mean`
(`analyze.py:71-74`), applied at `probe_s3.py:290` to the estimator's

```python
def signed_mean(hat: np.ndarray, ref: np.ndarray, roi: np.ndarray) -> float:
    h = hat.ravel()[roi]
    r = ref.ravel()[roi]
    return float(((h - r) / r).mean())
```

In words, the coded rule is: **b(S3) = the mean over the three S3 seeds {0,1,2}
at N=4M of the per-run signed mean b = mean_ROI[(X_p − ref_p)/ref_p]** (both
sides mass-1 normalized, study top-decile ROI); **SE(b) = |b(S0 seed 0) −
b(S0 seed 1)|/√2 from the FIRST TWO sorted seeds of the same-budget N=4M S0
ladder** (the third S0 seed is not used in the SE); **|b|/SE for S3 is
|b(S3 seed-mean)| / that SE**; and the flag `S3_no_visible_skew` is
|b(S3)| ≤ 3·SE(b). The verdict's bias branch `crit_bias` then requires BOTH
(a) S3 shows no visible skew under that 3·SE flag AND (b) this session's
S2 |b|/SE ≥ 5 × max(S3 |b|/SE, 1e-12) — i.e. the branch can only fire in S3's
favor if S2 carries at least 5x S3's bias/SE excess while S3 itself sits within
3 SE of zero. Measured (JSON of record): flag False on both maps
(mandel SE_b = 0.0045893, |b(S3)|/SE = 23.28; cosmut SE_b = 3.3620e-05,
|b(S3)|/SE = 434.84; `S3_no_visible_skew: false`), so `crit_bias` = False on
both maps regardless of the S2 comparison (S2: 88.87 / 2958.77), and the
licensed verdict rested on the slope branch alone (also False on both maps) —
hence `refuted`, with the variance-explosion rule (e_N ratio ≥ 100 AND
matched-compute rel ratio > 1 on both match axes) additionally satisfied.
